# CARHENGE ✓

**Location:** Alliance, Nebraska

**What Makes It World's Largest:** America's most famous car Stonehenge replica. 38 vintage American automobiles (1950s-60s) arranged in 96-foot diameter circle, dimensions match Stonehenge proportions. All painted gray.

**Address:** 2151 County Rd 59, Alliance, NE 69301 [42.1423, -102.8580]

**Hours:** Dawn to dusk daily, year-round. Gift shop seasonal (typically Mon-Sat 10am-5pm summer).

**Fees:** FREE (donations encouraged)

**History:** Built summer 1987 by Jim Reinders (engineer/farmer) with ~35 family members as memorial to his father Herman, who lived on the farm. Reinders studied Stonehenge while living in England. Completed for Summer Solstice June 1987. 39 automobiles total, some trunk-down in 5-foot pits, others welded as arches. Heel stone: 1962 Cadillac. Three cars buried with sign: "Here lie three bones of foreign cars." Initially faced opposition from Alliance residents and city council ordered it torn down. "Friends of Carhenge" formed to save it. Now city proudly proclaims "Home of Carhenge." 1992: Reinders removed Toyota, saying "We wanted American Carhenge, not Japanese." 2006: Visitor center built. 2013: Friends of Carhenge donated site to Citizens of Alliance. Featured in Pixar's "Cars on the Road" series. Became major destination during 2017 solar eclipse (in path of totality).

**Additional Car Art:** Site now includes "Car Art Reserve" with additional sculptures:
- "Fourd Seasons" (by Jim Reinders, uses Fords to represent Nebraska's seasonal wheat cycle)
- "Spawning Salmon" (by Geoff Sandhurst, Canada - won $2,500 prize)
- Other car art sculptures

**Seasonal Information:** Accessible year-round. Spectacular at night with flashlights (no lighting). Can autograph car on hill with permanent marker.

**Status:** Active, well-maintained. [LIMITED ACCESS] Remote location: 80+ miles from nearest interstate, deep in western Nebraska panhandle, 3 miles north of Alliance on Highway 87.

**Reinders' Note from Journal (June 20, 1987):** "Our schedule called for finishing Carhenge today. We were able to reduce the time of the original Stonehenge construction by 1999 years and 51 weeks. Now either they did not have a backhoe and forklift or they were not as well organized as we were."
