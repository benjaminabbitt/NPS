# America's Largest: The Complete Guide to World's Largest Tourist Attractions

**Last Updated: November 2025**

**SCOPE:** This comprehensive guide documents 120+ "world's largest" attractions across the United States, including verified records, self-declared claims, contested titles, closed/deteriorated sites, and minimally accessible attractions. Each entry includes notations regarding verification status and accessibility.

**LEGEND:**
- ✓ = Guinness World Record or independently verified
- [SELF-DECLARED] = Claim not independently verified
- [CONTESTED] = Multiple sites claim same title
- [CLOSED] = No longer operating/accessible
- [DETERIORATED] = Exists but condition compromised
- [LIMITED ACCESS] = Difficult to reach or restricted access

## NATURAL FORMATIONS & WONDERS

### MAMMOTH CAVE NATIONAL PARK, Kentucky ✓
**World's Longest Cave System**

**What Makes It World's Largest:** 426+ miles of surveyed passageways - 1.5 times longer than second-longest cave system globally

**Address:** 1 Mammoth Cave Parkway, Mammoth Cave, KY 42259
**GPS:** 37.1862° N, 86.1000° W

**Hours:**
- Park grounds: Open 24/7 year-round
- Visitor Center: 8:00 AM - 6:00 PM (seasonal variations)
- Cave tours: Require advance reservations via recreation.gov or 877-444-6777

**Fees:**
- Park entrance: Free
- Cave tours: $6-$60 depending on tour type and length
- Reservations strongly recommended

**History:** Native Americans explored approximately 5,000 years ago. European discovery around 1797. Formed in 325-350 million-year-old Mississippian limestone. Became National Park July 1, 1941. UNESCO World Heritage Site (1981) and International Biosphere Reserve (1990).

**Seasonal Information:** Best visited spring through fall. Cave maintains constant 54°F year-round. Peak visitation summer months and holiday weekends. Some surface trails challenging in winter though cave tours continue.

**Source:** National Park Service

### GENERAL SHERMAN TREE, California ✓
**World's Largest Tree by Volume**

**What Makes It World's Largest:** Largest living single-stem tree on Earth at 52,500 cubic feet. Height 275 feet, base diameter 36 feet, circumference over 102 feet.

**Address:** Sequoia National Park, 47050 Generals Highway, Three Rivers, CA 93271
**GPS:** 36.5816° N, 118.7516° W

**Hours:** Park open year-round, 24 hours. Lodgepole Visitor Center typically 8:00 AM - 5:00 PM.

**Access:** Half-mile paved trail from parking lot (downhill to tree, uphill return). Wheelchair-accessible alternative trail available from disability parking.

**Fees:**
- Park entrance: $35 per vehicle (7-day pass)
- America the Beautiful Annual Pass: $80 (all National Parks)

**History:** Estimated 2,200-2,700 years old. Named 1879 by naturalist James Wolverton after General William Tecumseh Sherman. Officially identified as world's largest 1931. Protected by Sequoia National Park since 1890.

**Seasonal Information:** Best May-October for easiest access. Snow possible October-June; roads may require chains. Peak season summer - arrive early for parking.

**Source:** National Park Service, Wikipedia

### HYPERION - WORLD'S TALLEST TREE, California ✓ [LIMITED ACCESS]
**World's Tallest Known Living Tree**

**What Makes It World's Largest:** 380.3 feet tall (115.92 meters) coast redwood

**Location:** Redwood National Park, California (exact location undisclosed)

**Access:** **CLOSED TO PUBLIC** - Exact location kept secret to prevent damage. $5,000 fine and up to 6 months jail time for attempting to find it. General park access available.

**History:** Discovered August 25, 2006 by naturalists Chris Atkins and Michael Taylor. Located in remote area of park. Named after Greek Titan. Coast redwood (Sequoia sempervirens), estimated 600-800 years old.

**Why Secret:** Heavy foot traffic causes soil compaction and root damage. Previous publicized tall trees suffered significant damage from visitors.

**Seasonal Information:** Park open year-round. Visitors can see other tall trees on designated trails.

**Fees:** Park entrance $30 per vehicle (7-day pass)

**Source:** National Park Service, Redwood Parks

### GRAND PRISMATIC SPRING, Wyoming ✓
**Largest Hot Spring in United States**

**What Makes It World's Largest:** Largest hot spring in US, third-largest globally. Diameter 370 feet, depth over 160 feet, temperature 147-189°F, discharges approximately 560 gallons per minute.

**Address:** Yellowstone National Park, Midway Geyser Basin
**GPS:** 44.5247° N, 110.8378° W

**Hours:** Park open year-round. Boardwalk accessible during park hours. Best viewing late morning to early afternoon for clearest colors.

**Fees:**
- Park entrance: $35 per vehicle (7-day pass)
- $30 motorcycle; $20 per person (foot/bicycle)
- Annual pass: $70 for Yellowstone only

**History:** First recorded 1839 by fur trappers. Named 1871 by Hayden Geological Survey for prismatic colors. Part of Yellowstone's 640,000-year-old caldera system. Colors created by thermophilic bacteria and algae in different temperature zones.

**Seasonal Information:** Best late spring through early fall (May-September). Summer shows peak colors but crowded. Winter steam can obscure views. Grand Prismatic Spring Overlook provides 0.6-mile round-trip hike for aerial views.

**Source:** USGS, National Park Service

### YOSEMITE FALLS, California ✓
**Tallest Waterfall in North America**

**What Makes It World's Largest:** 2,425 feet total drop (Upper Fall 1,430 ft, Middle Cascades 675 ft, Lower Fall 320 ft)

**Address:** Yosemite National Park, Yosemite Valley, CA
**GPS (Lower Falls):** 37.7440° N, 119.5968° W

**Hours:** Park open 24 hours year-round. Lower Falls Trail: 1-mile loop, paved, wheelchair accessible. Upper Falls Trail: 7.2-mile round trip, strenuous (2,700 ft elevation gain).

**Fees:**
- Park entrance: $35 per vehicle (7-day pass)
- Reservation system required April-October for day use
- Timed entry reservation ($2) plus entrance fee peak season

**History:** Indigenous name "Choo-look" meaning "the fall." First survey 1913 by USGS. Valley carved during Pleistocene glaciation 2.6 million years ago.

**Seasonal Information:** Peak flow May (snowmelt season). Water flows November-July. Often completely dry July-October. "Moonbow" phenomenon visible during full moon in April-May.

**Source:** National Park Service

### EL CAPITAN, California ✓
**World's Largest Granite Monolith**

**What Makes It World's Largest:** Largest exposed granite monolith on Earth. Height 3,000 feet from base, summit elevation 7,569 feet, width approximately 1.5 miles at base.

**Address:** Yosemite National Park, Yosemite Valley, CA
**GPS:** 37.7341° N, 119.6374° W

**Hours:** Park open 24 hours year-round. Viewing always accessible. Trail to summit: 17-mile roundtrip, 2,900 ft elevation gain (10-15 hours).

**Fees:**
- Park entrance: $35 per vehicle (7-day pass)
- Reservation system required April-October
- Climbing: No permit required

**History:** Native name "Tutokanula" (Rock Chief). Spanish "El Capitán" given 1851. Approximately 100 million years old. First ascent 1958. First free solo climb 2017 by Alex Honnold (film "Free Solo").

**Seasonal Information:** Best April-October. Spring features Horsetail Fall "firefall" phenomenon (mid-February). Best views from El Capitan Meadow and Tunnel View.

**Source:** Wikipedia, National Park Service

### STEAMBOAT GEYSER, Wyoming ✓
**World's Tallest Active Geyser**

**What Makes It World's Largest:** World's tallest currently active geyser. Major eruptions 300-400+ feet (record 403 feet in 2019). Water phase 3-40 minutes; steam phase can last hours to 2+ days.

**Address:** Yellowstone National Park, Norris Geyser Basin, WY
**GPS:** 44.7256° N, 110.7032° W

**Hours:** Park and basin accessible 24/7 weather permitting. Norris Visitor Center seasonal 9:00 AM - 5:00 PM. Boardwalk approximately 0.5 miles from parking.

**Fees:**
- Park entrance: $35 per vehicle (7-day pass)
- Stay on boardwalks - thermal waters cause severe burns

**History:** Likely formed around 1878. Named for steam phase. Dormant 1911-1961 (50 years). Active phases 1960s, 1980s, 2018-present. Recent activity: 32 eruptions (2018), 48 (2019), 48 (2020).

**Seasonal Information:** Accessible year-round weather dependent. Eruptions unpredictable (intervals 3 days to 50 years). Monitor GeyserTimes.org for reports.

**Source:** Wikipedia, National Park Service

### CARLSBAD CAVERNS BIG ROOM, New Mexico ✓
**Largest Cave Chamber in North America**

**What Makes It World's Largest:** Largest single cave chamber in North America by volume. Dimensions: 4,000 feet long × 625 feet wide × 255 feet high. Floor area 8.2 acres.

**Address:** 727 Carlsbad Caverns Highway, Carlsbad, NM 88220
**GPS:** 32.1477° N, 104.4435° W

**Hours:**
- Park: 8:00 AM - 5:00 PM daily (ticket sales close 3:30 PM)
- Closed Christmas Day only
- Big Room Trail: 1.25-mile loop, 1.5 hours

**Access:** Natural Entrance Trail (1.25 miles, steep 750 ft descent) or Elevator. Temperature constant 56°F - bring jacket.

**Fees:**
- Entrance: $15 per person (16+); children under 15 free
- Reservations required via recreation.gov
- Audio guide $5
- Closed-toe shoes required

**History:** Known to Native Americans 1,000+ years. Modern exploration 1898. Capitan Reef limestone 250 million years old. Cave formation 4-6 million years ago. National Monument 1923, National Park 1930, UNESCO World Heritage 1995.

**Seasonal Information:** Best spring and fall. Bat Flight Program May-October at sunset.

**Source:** USGS, National Park Service

### DELICATE ARCH, Utah
**Most Famous Natural Arch, Largest Freestanding in Park**

**What Makes It World's Largest:** Largest freestanding arch in Arches National Park. Height 52 feet, opening 46 feet high × 32 feet wide. Featured on Utah license plates.

**Address:** Arches National Park, PO Box 907, Moab, UT 84532
Trailhead: Wolfe Ranch Road, 11.7 miles from entrance
**GPS:** 38.7444° N, 109.4992° W

**Hours:** Park open 24 hours. Visitor Center 9:00 AM - 4:00 PM. Trail: 3-mile round trip, strenuous, 480 ft gain, 2-3 hours.

**Fees:**
- Park entrance: $30 per vehicle (7-day pass)
- Timed entry required April-October ($2 reservation)
- Bring 1 liter water per person minimum

**History:** Formed from Entrada Sandstone (Jurassic, ~140 million years). Featured on Utah quarter 2014. Climbing banned 2006.

**Seasonal Information:** Best spring (April-May) and fall (September-October). Summer extreme heat - hike early/late. Sunset most popular.

**Source:** National Park Service

### LANDSCAPE ARCH, Utah ✓
**Longest Natural Arch in United States**

**What Makes It World's Largest:** Longest natural arch span in US, 5th longest globally. Span: 290.1 feet ± 0.8 feet. Height 77.5 feet, width 18 feet, thickness only 6 feet at thinnest.

**Address:** Arches National Park, Devils Garden area
Trailhead: Devils Garden parking (18 miles from visitor center)
**GPS:** 38.7838° N, 109.6061° W

**Hours:** Park open 24 hours. Trail: 1.6 miles round trip, relatively flat, 30-45 minutes each way.

**Fees:**
- Park entrance: $30 per vehicle
- Timed entry April-October
- Viewing only - cannot pass beneath (trail closed for safety)

**History:** Named 1933-34 by Frank Beckwith. Major rockfalls 1991 and 1995 totaling 147 feet of rock. Extremely fragile.

**Seasonal Information:** Best spring and fall. Summer very hot. Possible snow/ice winter.

**Source:** National Park Service

### DEVILS TOWER, Wyoming ✓
**America's First National Monument**

**What Makes It World's Largest:** World's largest and most spectacular example of columnar jointing. Height 867 feet above base, 1,267 feet above river. Summit 180 ft × 300 ft (1.5 acres). Columns up to 20 feet wide, some 600 feet tall.

**Address:** Devils Tower National Monument, PO Box 10, Devils Tower, WY 82714
**GPS:** 44.5902° N, 104.7145° W

**Hours:**
- Monument: Open 24 hours year-round
- Visitor Center: Typically 9:00 AM - 5:00 PM (closed winter)
- Tower Trail: 1.3-mile paved loop (45 minutes-1 hour)

**Fees:**
- Entrance: $25 per vehicle (7-day pass)
- Camping: Belle Fourche Campground $20/night
- Climbing: No permit required but register at ranger station

**History:** Sacred site to 20+ Northern Plains tribes for thousands of years. Indigenous names include Matȟó Thípila (Bear Lodge). First US National Monument (September 24, 1906 by Theodore Roosevelt). Igneous intrusion ~40.5 million years ago. First recorded climb July 4, 1893.

**Seasonal Information:** Best April-October. Voluntary climbing closure month of June during sacred tribal ceremonies. Featured in "Close Encounters of the Third Kind" (1977).

**Source:** National Park Service

### HOOVER DAM, Nevada/Arizona ✓
**Among Largest Concrete Arch-Gravity Dams in U.S.**

**What Makes It World's Largest:** 726 feet tall, 1,244 feet long, 660 feet thick at base. 3 million cubic yards concrete. Creates Lake Mead (largest U.S. reservoir by volume).

**Address:** U.S. Highway 93, Boulder City, NV 89005 (30 miles SE of Las Vegas)

**Hours:**
- Visitor Center: Daily 9am-5pm (closed Thanksgiving/Christmas)
- Guided Dam Tour: $40 (1 hour)
- Power Plant Tour: $25 adults, $15 ages 4-16
- Visitor Center only: $15 adults
- Parking: $10 per vehicle

**History:** Construction 1931-1936, dedicated by FDR September 30, 1935. Built during Great Depression. Provides water to 20 million people. 7+ million visitors annually.

**Seasonal Information:** Open year-round. Summer extreme temperatures. December 1-February 27: Night closures Monday-Friday (7pm-5am) for maintenance.

**Source:** Bureau of Reclamation

### BALLS OF THINGS

#### WORLD'S LARGEST BALL OF SISAL TWINE ✓
**Location:** Cawker City, Kansas

**What Makes It World's Largest:** Community-built ball; 46 feet circumference, 27,017+ pounds, 8,507,430+ feet of twine (~1,600+ miles), 14.6 feet diameter

**Address:** 804 Locust St, Cawker City, KS 67430 [39.5092, -98.4337]

**Hours:** Visible 24/7; call Linda Clover to add twine

**Fees:** FREE

**History:** Started December 24, 1953 by Kansas farmer Frank Stoeber. Moved to town for 1961 Centennial. Guinness certified 1973; Frank died 1974. Lost record to Minnesota 1978. Cawker City began annual "Twine-a-thon" 1982. Public can add twine year-round.

**Seasonal Information:** Accessible year-round. Annual Twine-a-thon in August.

**Status:** Active, well-maintained under protective shelter. Only sisal twine can be added. CCTV monitors prevent climbing.

**Source:** RoadsideAmerica.com

#### WORLD'S LARGEST BALL OF TWINE (Rolled by One Man) ✓ [CONTESTED]
**Location:** Darwin, Minnesota

**What Makes It World's Largest:** 12 feet diameter, 40 feet circumference, 17,400 pounds - **created by ONE MAN only** (this is the distinction from Cawker City's community ball)

**Address:** 1st St, Darwin, MN 55324 [45.0986, -94.4103]

**Hours:** Visible 24/7 (enclosed in glass gazebo); Museum varies (typically summer daily)

**Fees:** FREE; donations accepted at museum

**History:** Created by Francis A. Johnson starting March 1950. Worked 4 hours daily for 29 years. Stopped 1979 due to emphysema. Used railroad jack to rotate. Featured in "Weird Al" Yankovic's 1989 song. Town renamed street "Weird Alley" in Yankovic's honor (2019).

**Seasonal Information:** Museum limited winter hours; ball visible year-round.

**Status:** Preserved as Francis left it 1979 - no additions allowed. Protected in gazebo. Town celebrates "Twine Ball Day" second Saturday in August.

**Note:** Distinction from Cawker City: Darwin's rolled by one person vs. community effort.

**Source:** RoadsideAmerica.com

### CARHENGE ✓
**Location:** Alliance, Nebraska

**What Makes It World's Largest:** America's most famous car Stonehenge replica. 38 vintage American automobiles (1950s-60s) arranged in 96-foot diameter circle, dimensions match Stonehenge proportions. All painted gray.

**Address:** 2151 County Rd 59, Alliance, NE 69301 [42.1423, -102.8580]

**Hours:** Dawn to dusk daily, year-round. Gift shop seasonal (typically Mon-Sat 10am-5pm summer).

**Fees:** FREE (donations encouraged)

**History:** Built summer 1987 by Jim Reinders (engineer/farmer) with ~35 family members as memorial to his father Herman, who lived on the farm. Reinders studied Stonehenge while living in England. Completed for Summer Solstice June 1987. 39 automobiles total, some trunk-down in 5-foot pits, others welded as arches. Heel stone: 1962 Cadillac. Three cars buried with sign: "Here lie three bones of foreign cars." Initially faced opposition from Alliance residents and city council ordered it torn down. "Friends of Carhenge" formed to save it. Now city proudly proclaims "Home of Carhenge." 1992: Reinders removed Toyota, saying "We wanted American Carhenge, not Japanese." 2006: Visitor center built. 2013: Friends of Carhenge donated site to Citizens of Alliance. Featured in Pixar's "Cars on the Road" series. Became major destination during 2017 solar eclipse (in path of totality).

**Additional Car Art:** Site now includes "Car Art Reserve" with additional sculptures:
- "Fourd Seasons" (by Jim Reinders, uses Fords to represent Nebraska's seasonal wheat cycle)
- "Spawning Salmon" (by Geoff Sandhurst, Canada - won $2,500 prize)
- Other car art sculptures

**Seasonal Information:** Accessible year-round. Spectacular at night with flashlights (no lighting). Can autograph car on hill with permanent marker.

**Status:** Active, well-maintained. [LIMITED ACCESS] Remote location: 80+ miles from nearest interstate, deep in western Nebraska panhandle, 3 miles north of Alliance on Highway 87.

**Reinders' Note from Journal (June 20, 1987):** "Our schedule called for finishing Carhenge today. We were able to reduce the time of the original Stonehenge construction by 1999 years and 51 weeks. Now either they did not have a backhoe and forklift or they were not as well organized as we were."

### WORLD'S LARGEST CUCKOO CLOCK ✓ [CONTESTED]
**Location:** Sugarcreek, Ohio

**What Makes It World's Largest:** 23 feet tall, 24 feet wide. Every half hour: cuckoo bird pops out, followed by 5-piece robot oomph-pa band and dancing couple performing to Swiss polka music.

**Address:** Corner of Main Street and N. Broadway, Sugarcreek, OH 44681

**Hours:** Performs every half hour daily. Does not operate in winter months. Always visible.

**Fees:** FREE

**History:** Commissioned by Alpine-Alpa restaurant (Wilmot, OH) in 1963. Took 12 years and $50,000 to build by clockmaker Karl Schleutermann. Featured on cover of Guinness Book of World Records 1977. Stood at restaurant for 30+ years. Restaurant closed 2009. Walnut Creek Cheese purchased clock and donated to Village of Sugarcreek 2010. Moved to current location May 2012. Fully restored 2007 (Hampton Hotel "Save-A-Landmark" program). Restored again by volunteers 2012 to working condition. Historical marker erected 2015 by Village of Sugarcreek and Ohio History Connection.

**Seasonal Information:** Performs daily spring through fall. Winter: no performances (mechanisms protected from cold), but clock always visible.

**Status:** Active. Well-maintained centerpiece of "Little Switzerland of Ohio" town. Sugarcreek offers Swiss/German heritage, cheese shops, Amish country.

**Note:** Contested - other cuckoo clocks have since claimed to be larger, but this held Guinness record in 1977.

**Source:** Visit Sugarcreek, World Record Academy, Historical Marker Database

## TRIP PLANNING ESSENTIALS

### SEASONAL CONSIDERATIONS

**Spring (March-May):**
- Best time for most outdoor attractions - moderate weather, smaller crowds
- Peak flow season for waterfalls (especially Yosemite Falls)
- Good time for Southwest desert attractions before extreme heat

**Summer (June-August):**
- Peak season for most attractions - expect crowds
- National Parks require advance reservations (Yellowstone, Yosemite, Arches)
- Extreme heat in desert regions (100°F+) - visit early morning or evening
- Best time for northern attractions (North Dakota, Minnesota)
- Bat Flight Programs active at Carlsbad Caverns
- Most seasonal roadside attractions' gift shops open

**Fall (September-November):**
- Ideal visiting time - moderate temperatures, fewer crowds, fall foliage
- Many festivals celebrate roadside attractions
- Good time for Southern attractions before winter closures

**Winter (December-February):**
- Many northern attractions difficult to access but technically viewable
- Reduced hours at museums and visitor centers
- Cave attractions excellent year-round (constant temperature)
- Lower crowds at Southwest attractions
- Some attractions don't operate (Ohio Cuckoo Clock)

### PRACTICAL TIPS

**Reservations & Planning:**
- National Parks: Book 3-6 months in advance for peak season
- Timed entry systems common at popular parks
- Many roadside attractions require no reservations (24/7 viewing)
- Call ahead for private attractions (Ball of Paint, museum hours)
- Dog Bark Park Inn B&B is CLOSED (2024) but structure viewable

**Cost Considerations:**
- Most roadside attractions: FREE
- National Parks: $25-35 per vehicle (7-day pass)
- America the Beautiful Annual Pass: $80 (all National Parks)
- Museums and indoor attractions: $10-50
- Budget $50-100 per day for family admissions

**Photography Tips:**
- Golden hour (sunrise/sunset) best for outdoor attractions
- Roadside attractions often well-lit at night
- Bring wide-angle lens for large structures
- Respect "no climbing" signs - many attractions monitor with cameras
- Best time for Spoonbridge & Cherry: daytime for colors
- Carhenge at night with flashlights = spectacular experience

**Safety & Etiquette:**
- Stay on designated trails and boardwalks (especially thermal areas)
- Bring adequate water (1 liter per person minimum for hikes)
- Check weather conditions for northern attractions in winter
- Respect private property and operating hours
- Many attractions have donation boxes - contributions appreciated
- Hyperion Tree: DO NOT attempt to find it - $5,000 fine + jail time

**Accessibility:**
- Most roadside attractions viewable from parking areas
- National Park visitor centers typically wheelchair accessible
- Call ahead for specific accessibility needs
- Many caves have elevator access (Carlsbad Caverns)
- Some attractions require hiking (Delicate Arch - strenuous)
