## Key Activities

- **Theatre and Presidential Box** (30 minutes) - View the historic theatre on a self-guided tour and see the President's Box from the balcony where Abraham Lincoln was shot on April 14, 1865. National Park Service rangers are on hand to answer questions. Timed-entry tours are available every half hour from 9:00 AM to 4:30 PM. 511 10th Street NW, Washington, DC 20004; Daily 9:00 AM-5:00 PM; (202) 347-4833 [Source: [Ford's Theatre](https://fords.org/visit-us/historic-site-visit/), [TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g28970-d104861-Reviews-Ford_s_Theatre-Washington_DC_District_of_Columbia.html), [NPS Operating Hours](https://www.nps.gov/foth/planyourvisit/hours.htm)]

- **Ranger Presentation** (20-30 minutes) - A U.S. Park Ranger presents a talk on the stage of the historic theatre discussing the events leading up to and during Lincoln's assassination. All ranger talks take place in the historic theatre. 511 10th Street NW, Washington, DC 20004; Check schedule on arrival [Source: [Free Tours by Foot](https://freetoursbyfoot.com/fords-theatre-tour/), [NPS Event](https://www.nps.gov/planyourvisit/event-details.htm?id=92CAB25B-FF8E-2568-56617ECB297A5D62)]

- **Museum Exhibition** (15-30 minutes) - Start your self-guided tour in the basement museum beneath the theatre. The Lincoln Museum displays artifacts including the Derringer pistol used by John Wilkes Booth, Booth's diary, the original door to Lincoln's theater box, and Lincoln's coat. Exhibits detail the elaborate conspiracy planned by Booth. 511 10th Street NW, Washington, DC 20004; Daily 9:00 AM-5:00 PM [Source: [TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/FAQ_Answers-g28970-d104861-t3522800-About_how_long_does_it_take_to_see_Ford_s.html), [Trolley Tours](https://www.trolleytours.com/washington-dc/fords-theatre)]

- **Petersen House Tour** (15-20 minutes) - Visit the house directly across the street from Ford's Theatre where President Lincoln was carried after being shot and where he died the morning of April 15, 1865. The 19th-century federal style boarding house has been preserved and operated as a museum since the 1930s. 516 10th Street NW, Washington, DC 20004; Daily 8:30 AM-5:00 PM; Note: Strollers are not permitted inside the Petersen House [Source: [NPS Petersen House](https://www.nps.gov/foth/the-petersen-house.htm), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petersen_House), [NPS Guidelines](https://fords.org/visit-us/plan-your-visit/historic-site-visit-guidelines/)]

- **Aftermath Exhibits** (15-30 minutes) - Located in the Ford's Theatre Center for Education and Leadership across the street, these exhibits on the 2nd, 3rd and 4th floors cover the hunt for John Wilkes Booth, Lincoln's funeral train, and a nation in mourning. Interactive exhibits show the crowds that gathered each time the funeral train stopped. 10th Street NW, Washington, DC 20004; Daily 9:00 AM-5:00 PM [Source: [NP Plan](https://npplan.com/parks-by-state/washington-d-c-national-parks/fords-theatre-national-historic-site-park-at-a-glance/fords-theatre-national-historic-site-museums/fords-theatre-national-historic-site-center-for-education-and-leadership/), [TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g28970-d104861-Reviews-Ford_s_Theatre-Washington_DC_District_of_Columbia.html)]

## Hidden Gems

- **Baptist Alley** (10-15 minutes) - Walk around the block to the alley behind Ford's Theatre (accessible via F Street between 9th & 10th Streets NW) to see where John Wilkes Booth kept his horse and made his escape after the assassination. Before Ford bought the building in 1862, it housed the 10th Baptist Congregation, which gave the alley its name. Booth's horse dashed down Baptist Alley after the assassination, and he turned northward onto F Street. F Street between 9th & 10th Streets NW, Washington, DC 20004; Always accessible [Source: [Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/baptist-alley), [Lincoln Conspirators](https://lincolnconspirators.com/2014/07/17/john-wilkes-booths-movements-at-fords-theatre/)]

- **Surratt Boarding House Area** (Walking tour, 15-20 minutes) - A few blocks from Ford's Theatre is the former site of the Surratt boarding house where the assassination conspiracy was hatched. This is a lesser-known stop for those interested in the full story of the plot against Lincoln. H Street NW area, Washington, DC; Viewable from exterior [Source: [Washingtonian Hidden Gems](https://www.washingtonian.com/2020/01/26/92-hidden-gems-attractions-in-washington/)]

- **Lot Flannery's Lincoln Statue** (5-10 minutes) - A lesser-known marble statue of Lincoln stands outside what's now the DC Court of Appeals. The statue is significant because it was created by sculptor Lot Flannery, who was actually present at Ford's Theatre the night Lincoln was shot. 430 E Street NW, Washington, DC 20001; Exterior viewing always available [Source: [DC Travel Magazine](https://dctravelmag.com/hidden-gems-in-washington-dc/)]

## Also Nearby

- **National Portrait Gallery and Smithsonian American Art Museum** (2-3 hours, 4 minutes walk) - Both museums share the historic Donald W. Reynolds Center (Old Patent Office Building) and can be visited together. The museums showcase American art and portraits including the famous Lansdowne portrait of George Washington and the "Chalk" portrait of Lincoln. Admission is free. 8th and F Streets NW, Washington, DC 20001 (38.897797, -77.023132); Daily 11:30 AM-7:00 PM except December 25; Located above Gallery Place/Chinatown Metro station [Source: [Washington.org](https://washington.org/DC-guide-to/smithsonian-national-portrait-gallery), [Smithsonian](https://americanart.si.edu/visit/saam), [TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/AttractionsNear-g28970-d104861-Ford_s_Theatre-Washington_DC_District_of_Columbia.html)]

- **International Spy Museum** (2-3 hours, 15 minutes by Metro) - Interactive museum where visitors can test their spy skills through hands-on exhibits. The museum features extensive collections on espionage history and techniques. Most visitors spend 2-3 hours exploring, though those who engage deeply with all content can spend 3-4 hours. Tickets must be purchased in advance. 700 L'Enfant Plaza SW, Washington, DC 20024 (38.884, -77.025); Daily 9:00 AM-7:00 PM (extended hours on weekends until 8:00 PM), last ticket sold one hour before closing [Source: [Spy Museum](https://www.spymuseum.org/visit/), [Washington.org](https://washington.org/visit-dc/guide-to-international-spy-museum), [TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g28970-d254773-Reviews-International_Spy_Museum-Washington_DC_District_of_Columbia.html)]

- **President Lincoln's Cottage** (1-1.5 hours, 30 minutes by Metro/car) - An off-the-beaten-path site in Northwest DC where Lincoln spent about a quarter of his presidency, away from the White House. The cottage features authentically furnished interiors and charming gardens. Tours begin in the Visitor Education Center and last approximately 1 hour. Admission by guided tour only; advance ticket purchase strongly recommended as weekend tours often sell out. 140 Rock Creek Church Road NW (Eagle Gate entrance), Washington, DC 20011 (38.9417264, -77.0118516); Monday-Saturday: Visitor Center 9:30 AM-4:30 PM, Tours 10:00 AM-3:00 PM; Sunday: Visitor Center 10:30 AM-4:30 PM, Tours 11:00 AM-3:00 PM; Closed Thanksgiving, Christmas, and New Year's Day; (202) 829-0436 [Source: [Lincoln Cottage](https://www.lincolncottage.org/), [TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g28970-d581522-Reviews-President_Lincoln_s_Cottage-Washington_DC_District_of_Columbia.html), [Washington.org](https://washington.org/find-dc-listings/president-lincolns-cottage)]
