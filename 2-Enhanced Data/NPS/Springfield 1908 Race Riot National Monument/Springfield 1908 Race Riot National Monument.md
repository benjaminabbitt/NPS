# Springfield 1908 Race Riot National Monument

## Cancellation Stamp Locations

- **Lincoln Home National Historic Site Visitor Center** (426 South 7th Street, Springfield, IL 62701; 39.7973527, -89.6448765; Daily 9:00 AM - 5:00 PM; Phone: 217-492-4241) - The passport cancellation stamp for Springfield 1908 Race Riot National Monument is currently available at the Lincoln Home NHS Visitor Center, as the monument does not yet have its own dedicated visitor center. [Source: [NPS Springfield 1908](https://www.nps.gov/places/springfield-1908-race-riot-national-monument.htm)]

**Note:** This is a new national monument (established March 2024). Visitor services and stamp locations may change as the site develops. Call ahead to confirm stamp availability.

## Key Activities

- **1908 Race Riot Walking Tour** (1.5-2 hours) - A self-guided walking tour through the historic Badlands neighborhood covering significant sites from the August 1908 race riot that killed at least 17 people, destroyed 40 homes, and displaced hundreds of African American residents. The tour includes stops at the sites of Scott Burton's barbershop, William Donnegan's home (an 80-year-old Black man lynched by the mob), and locations where businesses were burned. The riot became the catalyst for the founding of the NAACP in 1909. Walking tour maps available at Lincoln Home NHS Visitor Center. Downtown Springfield, IL; accessible 24 hours. [Source: [NPS Springfield 1908](https://www.nps.gov/s1rr/index.htm), [WBEZ Chicago](https://www.wbez.org/stories/springfield-1908-race-riot-national-monument/7c9a0f06-d0db-4c6d-b1b9-f9f9c9d5f5d5)]

- **Monument Viewing and Reflection** (30-45 minutes) - Visit the memorial site commemorating the victims of the 1908 Springfield Race Riot. The monument honors those killed, injured, and displaced during the two-day riot that erupted on August 14-15, 1908. Interpretive panels explain the events, causes, and lasting impact of the riot on civil rights history in America. Corner of Madison Street and 9th Street, Springfield, IL; accessible daily. [Source: [NPS Places](https://www.nps.gov/places/springfield-1908-race-riot-national-monument.htm)]

- **Springfield and Central Illinois African American History Museum** (1-1.5 hours) - Located nearby, this museum provides essential context for understanding the African American experience in Springfield before, during, and after the 1908 riot. Exhibits cover the Great Migration, local civil rights leaders, and the community's recovery. 1440 Monument Avenue, Springfield, IL 62702; Wednesday-Saturday 12:00 PM - 4:00 PM; Phone: 217-391-6323. [Source: [SCIAAHM](https://www.springfieldaahistory.org/)]

- **Ranger-Led Programs** (45-60 minutes, seasonal) - When available, NPS rangers offer guided walks and talks about the 1908 Race Riot, its causes rooted in racial tensions and false accusations, and its significance in sparking the civil rights movement. Programs depart from Lincoln Home NHS Visitor Center. Check with the visitor center for current schedule. 426 South 7th Street, Springfield, IL; times vary seasonally. [Source: [NPS Springfield 1908](https://www.nps.gov/s1rr/planyourvisit/index.htm)]

- **Junior Ranger Program** (30-45 minutes) - Educational activities for young visitors to learn about the Springfield 1908 Race Riot and its historical significance. Booklets available at Lincoln Home NHS Visitor Center. Complete activities to earn a Junior Ranger badge. 426 South 7th Street, Springfield, IL; during visitor center hours. [Source: [NPS Springfield 1908](https://www.nps.gov/s1rr/learn/kidsyouth/index.htm)]

## Hidden Gems

- **Luminary Observance Ceremony** (1-2 hours, annual event) - Each August 14th, the Springfield community holds a luminary ceremony commemorating the anniversary of the riot. Luminaries are placed at significant sites throughout the historic Badlands neighborhood, and community members gather for readings, music, and remembrance. This deeply moving annual event connects visitors with the ongoing legacy of the tragedy. Various locations in downtown Springfield; August 14th annually, evening hours. [Source: [State Journal-Register](https://www.sj-r.com/)]

- **William Donnegan Historical Marker** (15-20 minutes) - A historical marker honors William Donnegan, an 80-year-old Black shoemaker and friend of Abraham Lincoln who was dragged from his home and lynched by the white mob. Donnegan had lived in Springfield for over 50 years and was married to a white woman, making him a target of the rioters' fury. The marker provides a personal connection to the tragedy. Spring Street near 7th Street, Springfield, IL; accessible 24 hours. [Source: [Illinois History](https://www.historyillinois.org/)]

- **Badlands Neighborhood Exploration** (45-60 minutes) - Explore what remains of the historic Badlands district, once a thriving African American neighborhood that was devastated during the riot. While most original structures are gone, walking the streets provides a contemplative experience understanding the physical and psychological destruction inflicted on the community. Area bounded by Washington, Jefferson, 7th, and 12th Streets, Springfield, IL. [Source: [NPS Springfield 1908](https://www.nps.gov/s1rr/learn/historyculture/index.htm)]

- **NAACP Founding Connection Exhibit** (20-30 minutes) - Learn how the Springfield riot directly led to the founding of the NAACP. Shocked by the violence occurring in Lincoln's hometown, a group of white progressives and Black activists including W.E.B. Du Bois, Ida B. Wells, and Mary White Ovington issued a call that became the founding of the National Association for the Advancement of Colored People on February 12, 1909 (Lincoln's 100th birthday). Information available at Lincoln Home NHS Visitor Center. [Source: [NAACP History](https://naacp.org/about/our-history)]

## Also Nearby

- **Lincoln Home National Historic Site** (1.5-2 hours, adjacent) - The restored home where Abraham Lincoln lived from 1844 to 1861 before becoming President. Tour the only home Lincoln ever owned, explore the historic four-block neighborhood, and visit the comprehensive visitor center. The connection between Lincoln's legacy and the race riot that occurred in his hometown decades later adds profound context to both sites. 413 South 8th Street, Springfield, IL 62701; Daily 9:00 AM - 5:00 PM; Free guided tours every 15-30 minutes. [Source: [NPS Lincoln Home](https://www.nps.gov/liho/index.htm)]

- **Abraham Lincoln Presidential Library and Museum** (2-3 hours, 0.5 miles) - One of the most visited presidential libraries in America, featuring immersive exhibits on Lincoln's life, presidency, and legacy. Special effects theaters, original artifacts including the Gettysburg Address copies, and exhibits on Mary Todd Lincoln. 212 North 6th Street, Springfield, IL 62701; Daily 9:00 AM - 5:00 PM; Admission charged. [Source: [ALPLM](https://presidentlincoln.illinois.gov/)]

- **Old State Capitol State Historic Site** (1-1.5 hours, 0.3 miles) - The building where Lincoln delivered his famous "House Divided" speech in 1858 and where his body lay in state in 1865. Beautifully restored to its 1858 appearance, the building served as Illinois' capitol from 1839 to 1876. 1 Old State Capitol Plaza, Springfield, IL 62701; Daily 9:00 AM - 5:00 PM; Free admission. [Source: [Illinois Historic Sites](https://www.illinoishistory.gov/)]

- **Lincoln Tomb State Historic Site** (45-60 minutes, 1.5 miles) - The final resting place of Abraham Lincoln, his wife Mary, and three of their four sons. The impressive tomb monument includes a bronze bust of Lincoln whose nose visitors rub for good luck. 1500 Monument Avenue, Springfield, IL 62702; Daily 9:00 AM - 5:00 PM; Free admission. [Source: [Illinois Historic Sites](https://www.lincolntomb.org/)]

- **Dana-Thomas House** (1-1.5 hours, 0.8 miles) - A masterpiece of Frank Lloyd Wright's Prairie School architecture, this 1902 home is one of the best-preserved and most complete of Wright's early designs. Features original Wright-designed furniture, art glass, and fixtures. 301 East Lawrence Avenue, Springfield, IL 62703; Wednesday-Sunday 9:00 AM - 4:00 PM; Admission charged. [Source: [Dana-Thomas House](https://dana-thomas.org/)]

## Total Recommended Time

**Total recommended visit time for Springfield 1908 Race Riot National Monument: 2-3 hours**

This includes 1.5-2 hours for the self-guided walking tour through the historic Badlands neighborhood, 30-45 minutes for monument viewing and reflection, and time for interpretive exhibits at the Lincoln Home NHS Visitor Center. The site is a new national monument (established March 2024) and visitor services are still developing. For a comprehensive civil rights history experience, combine with the Springfield and Central Illinois African American History Museum (add 1-1.5 hours) and Lincoln Home NHS (add 1.5-2 hours) for a full-day exploration of Springfield's complex history with race and equality.
