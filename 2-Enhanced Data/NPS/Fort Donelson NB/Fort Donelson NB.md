# Fort Donelson NB (National Battlefield)

## Cancellation Stamp Locations

- **Fort Donelson Visitor Center** (120 Fort Donelson Park Road, Dover, TN 37058; 36.4831363, -87.8625811; Daily 8:30 AM - 4:00 PM CST; Phone: 931-232-5706 x0) - The visitor center features exhibits, an Eastern National gift shop, and passport stamp station in the lobby. Closed on Thanksgiving, December 25, and January 1. [Source: [NPS Things to Do](https://www.nps.gov/fodo/planyourvisit/things2do.htm), [NPS Basic Info](https://www.nps.gov/fodo/planyourvisit/basicinfo.htm)]

**Note:** Free admission. Call 931-232-5706 x0 to confirm current hours and program availability.

## Key Activities

- **Visitor Center Exhibits & Films** (45-60 minutes) - Explore comprehensive exhibits about the February 1862 Battle of Fort Donelson, General Ulysses S. Grant's first major victory of the Civil War. Watch two orientation films: "Fort Donelson: Gateway to the Confederate Heartland" (17 minutes) and "The Historic Dover Hotel - Surrender House" (14 minutes). Both films feature audio description for accessibility. Eastern National bookstore on-site. 120 Fort Donelson Park Road, Dover, TN 37058; Daily 8:30 AM - 4:00 PM. [Source: [NPS Things to Do](https://www.nps.gov/fodo/planyourvisit/things2do.htm)]

- **Self-Guided Driving Tour** (1.5-2 hours) - Follow the auto tour route visiting key battlefield sites with wayside interpretive exhibits at every location. Parking available throughout for visitors to exit and explore on foot. The tour covers the Confederate river batteries, earthwork fortifications, and key battle positions. Use the NPS mobile app for enhanced interpretation. Park grounds; Tour road open 8:00 AM - 5:00 PM. [Source: [NPS Things to Do](https://www.nps.gov/fodo/planyourvisit/things2do.htm)]

- **Dover Hotel (Surrender House)** (20-30 minutes) - Visit the 1851 inn where Confederate General Simon Bolivar Buckner surrendered unconditionally to Union General Ulysses S. Grant on February 16, 1862. This was the first major Union victory of the Civil War and made Grant a national hero. The hotel has been restored to its 1862 appearance with period furnishings. 101 Petty Street, Dover, TN 37058; Daily during park hours; check at visitor center for interior access. [Source: [NPS History](https://www.nps.gov/fodo/learn/historyculture/index.htm)]

- **Confederate River Batteries** (30-45 minutes) - Walk among the preserved earthwork positions where Confederate artillery defended the Cumberland River approach. View replica cannons positioned overlooking the river where Confederate guns engaged Union gunboats. Interpretive signs explain the naval battle of February 14, 1862. Along driving tour route; accessible during park hours. [Source: [NPS History](https://www.nps.gov/fodo/learn/historyculture/index.htm)]

- **Fort Donelson National Cemetery** (20-30 minutes) - Visit the cemetery established in 1867 containing approximately 670 Union soldiers (512 unknown) who died in the battle and surrounding campaigns. Walk among the headstones and monuments honoring those who fell. Located adjacent to the visitor center. 174 National Cemetery Drive, Dover, TN 37058; Daily dawn to dusk. [Source: [NPS National Cemetery](https://www.nps.gov/fodo/learn/historyculture/nationalcemetery.htm)]

- **Hiking Trails** (1-3 hours depending on trail) - Explore several miles of trails through the battlefield landscape, including paths through the outer defensive works, rifle pits, and wooded areas where fierce fighting occurred. The trails provide a more immersive battlefield experience than the driving tour alone. Throughout park; sunrise to sunset. [Source: [NPS Plan Your Visit](https://www.nps.gov/fodo/planyourvisit/index.htm)]

- **Guided Programs** (1-2 hours, by arrangement) - Interpretive programs are available for schools, civic groups, and military groups. Arrange special tours with at least two weeks' notice by contacting park rangers at 931-232-5706. Programs provide in-depth interpretation of the battle and its significance. By arrangement. [Source: [NPS Things to Do](https://www.nps.gov/fodo/planyourvisit/things2do.htm)]

- **Junior Ranger Program** (30-60 minutes) - Complete activities to learn about the Battle of Fort Donelson, General Grant's "Unconditional Surrender" demand, and Civil War military life. Earn a Junior Ranger badge upon completion. Available at visitor center during operating hours. [Source: [NPS Plan Your Visit](https://www.nps.gov/fodo/planyourvisit/index.htm)]

## Hidden Gems

- **Outer Earthwork Fortifications** (30-45 minutes) - Explore the extensive Confederate earthwork defensive line that stretched for nearly three miles around the fort. These well-preserved trenches and rifle pits show how soldiers dug in and fought in bitter February cold. Less visited than the river batteries but equally important to understanding the battle. Various locations along park trails; sunrise to sunset. [Source: [NPS History](https://www.nps.gov/fodo/learn/historyculture/index.htm)]

- **Grant's Headquarters Site** (15-20 minutes) - Visit the site where General Grant established his field headquarters during the siege and received General Buckner's request for surrender terms. Grant's famous reply demanding "unconditional surrender" gave him his nickname "Unconditional Surrender Grant." Marked along driving tour; accessible during park hours. [Source: [NPS History](https://www.nps.gov/fodo/learn/historyculture/index.htm)]

- **February Battle Anniversary Programs** (varies, mid-February) - The park hosts special commemorative programs around the anniversary of the battle (February 12-16). Events may include living history demonstrations, guided battlefield walks, and special ranger programs. Check the park calendar for specific dates and events. Mid-February annually. [Source: [NPS Calendar](https://www.nps.gov/fodo/planyourvisit/calendar.htm)]

- **Cumberland River Views** (15-20 minutes) - Take in scenic views of the Cumberland River from the battery positions. The strategic importance of controlling this waterway becomes clear from these vantage points. Understanding the river's role is key to understanding why this battle mattered. River battery overlooks; accessible during park hours. [Source: [NPS History](https://www.nps.gov/fodo/learn/historyculture/index.htm)]

## Also Nearby

- **Land Between the Lakes National Recreation Area** (2-6 hours, 10-15 miles) - A 170,000-acre peninsula between Kentucky Lake and Lake Barkley offering outdoor recreation including hiking, camping, fishing, hunting, and wildlife viewing. Features The Homeplace 1850s Living History Farm, Elk & Bison Prairie drive-through, and Golden Pond Planetarium. 238 Visitor Center Drive, Golden Pond, KY 42211; Open year-round; various activity hours. [Source: [Land Between the Lakes](https://www.landbetweenthelakes.us/)]

- **Fort Henry** (30-45 minutes, 15 miles west) - Site of the Confederate fort captured by Union forces on February 6, 1862, just ten days before Fort Donelson fell. Now mostly underwater due to Kentucky Lake, but interpretive markers explain its significance. Grant's army marched overland from here to attack Fort Donelson. Near New Johnsonville, TN; accessible via Land Between the Lakes. [Source: [NPS Fort Henry](https://www.nps.gov/fodo/learn/historyculture/fort-henry.htm)]

- **Cross Creeks National Wildlife Refuge** (1-2 hours, 10 miles) - A 8,862-acre refuge along Kentucky Lake providing habitat for wintering waterfowl and bald eagles. Wildlife observation, photography, hiking trails, and environmental education programs available. 643 Wildlife Road, Dover, TN 37058; Daily dawn to dusk; free. [Source: [USFWS Cross Creeks](https://www.fws.gov/refuge/cross-creeks)]

- **Clarksville** (1-2 hours, 30 miles southeast) - Historic Tennessee city with downtown shops, restaurants, and the Customs House Museum & Cultural Center. The city served as a strategic location during the Civil War. Downtown Clarksville, TN; Business hours vary. [Source: [Visit Clarksville](https://www.visitclarksvilletn.com/)]

## Total Recommended Time

**Total recommended visit time for Fort Donelson NB: 2-3 hours**

This includes 45-60 minutes for the visitor center exhibits and films, 1.5-2 hours for the self-guided driving tour with stops at key sites including the Dover Hotel, river batteries, and national cemetery. For a more comprehensive visit including hiking trails and detailed exploration, plan 4-5 hours. The site is free to enter and open daily.

[Sources: [NPS Plan Your Visit](https://www.nps.gov/fodo/planyourvisit/index.htm), [NPS Things to Do](https://www.nps.gov/fodo/planyourvisit/things2do.htm), [NPS Basic Info](https://www.nps.gov/fodo/planyourvisit/basicinfo.htm)]
