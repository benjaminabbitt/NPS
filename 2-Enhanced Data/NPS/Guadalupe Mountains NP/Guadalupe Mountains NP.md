## Key Activities

- **Guadalupe Peak Trail** (6-8 hours) - Hike to the highest point in Texas at 8,751 feet; this very strenuous 8.4-mile round trip gains 3,000 feet of elevation over steep, rocky terrain with spectacular summit views; start early to avoid afternoon thunderstorms and bring at least one gallon of water per person; Trailhead at Pine Springs; Daily 6:00 AM-midnight [Source: [NPS Guadalupe Peak](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/gumo_hike_guadalupe_peak.htm), [AllTrails](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/texas/guadalupe-peak-texas-highpoint-trail), [Earth Trekkers Guide](https://www.earthtrekkers.com/how-to-hike-to-guadalupe-peak/)]

- **McKittrick Canyon Trail to Pratt Cabin** (2.5-3 hours) - Hike the most popular 4.8-mile round trip segment through one of the park's most scenic canyons featuring deciduous trees that create spectacular fall colors in late October to early November; mostly flat terrain with minimal elevation gain; McKittrick Canyon Trailhead; Gate open 8:00 AM-4:30 PM MST [Source: [NPS Fall Colors](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/gumo-fall-colors.htm), [McKittrick Info](https://www.nps.gov/gumo/planyourvisit/mckittrick.htm), [Hiking Guide](https://visitguadalupemountains.com/hiking-mckittrick-canyon-trail/)]

- **Devil's Hall Trail** (3-5 hours) - Complete this moderate-to-strenuous 4.2-mile round trip hike featuring boulder scrambling through a rocky wash to reach the natural limestone Hiker's Staircase and Devil's Hall, a narrow passage between 100-foot limestone walls; Pine Springs Trailhead; Daily 6:00 AM-midnight [Source: [NPS Devil's Hall](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/gumo_hike_devils_hall_trail.htm), [AllTrails](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/texas/devils-hall), [Earth Trekkers Guide](https://www.earthtrekkers.com/devils-hall-trail-guadalupe-mountains/)]

- **Smith Spring Trail Loop** (1-2 hours) - Enjoy this easy-to-moderate 2.3-2.5 mile loop visiting two desert springs (Manzanita and Smith) with partially paved trails and 402 feet of elevation gain; Frijole Ranch Trailhead, 1 mile north of Pine Springs; Daily 6:00 AM-midnight [Source: [NPS Smith Spring](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/gumo_hike_smith_springs_trail.htm), [AllTrails](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/texas/smith-spring-and-manzanita-spring-loop), [Hiking Guide](https://visitguadalupemountains.com/hiking-smith-spring-trail/)]

- **El Capitan Trail** (4-5 hours) - Trek this moderate 9.1-mile round trip to the base of the iconic El Capitan peak with 1,656 feet of elevation gain and stunning valley views; note that the trail does not summit El Capitan itself; Pine Springs Trailhead; Daily 6:00 AM-midnight [Source: [AllTrails](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/texas/el-capitan-trail), [Hiking Guide](https://visitguadalupemountains.com/hiking-el-capitan-trail/), [Earth Trekkers](https://www.earthtrekkers.com/best-things-to-do-in-guadalupe-mountains-national-park/)]

- **Salt Basin Dunes Trail** (1-3 hours) - Walk the flat 3-mile round trip to gypsum sand dunes reaching 60 feet high; best visited at sunrise or sunset for dramatic lighting; requires 4WD vehicle to access trailhead via rough roads; West side of park off Williams Ranch Road; Daylight hours [Source: [NPS Salt Basin](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/gumo_salt_basin_dunes_trail.htm), [AllTrails](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/texas/guadalupe-salt-basin-dunes), [Hiking Guide](https://visitguadalupemountains.com/hiking-salt-basin-dunes-trail/)]

- **Pinery Trail** (30 minutes) - Walk this easy 0.75-mile paved, wheelchair-accessible path to the ruins of Pinery Station, the highest and most isolated stop on the historic 2,800-mile Butterfield Overland Mail Route from 1858-1859; Pine Springs Visitor Center; Daily 8:00 AM-4:00 PM [Source: [NPS The Pinery](https://www.nps.gov/places/gumo_the_pinery.htm), [Pinery Trail Info](https://inspiredimperfection.com/adventures/pinery-trail/), [Parkcation Guide](https://www.parkcation.com/nationalparks/guadalupemountains/pinerystation/)]

- **Frijole Ranch Cultural Museum** (30-60 minutes) - Explore this 1876 ranch house and grounds featuring seven historic buildings including a schoolhouse, bunkhouse, and barn; houses interpretive exhibits about ranching history; 1.5 miles northeast of Pine Springs Visitor Center; Seasonally open 8:00 AM-4:30 PM [Source: [NPS Frijole Ranch](https://www.nps.gov/places/gumo_frijole_ranch_museum.htm), [Museum Guide](https://visitguadalupemountains.com/frijole-ranch-museum/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frijole_Ranch)]

- **Night Sky Stargazing** (1-2 hours) - Experience Bortle class 2 exceptionally dark skies ranked #7 in America for stargazing; attend ranger-facilitated programs at Pine Springs Amphitheatre at 7:30 PM or stargaze independently to view constellations like Orion, Ursa Major, and Scorpius; Pine Springs area; Evening hours [Source: [Dark Sky Info](https://www.go-astronomy.com/dark-sky-park.php?Park=620), [Stargazing Rankings](https://secrethouston.com/texas-park-stargazing/), [Dark Sky Overland](https://darkskyoverland.com/overlander-guide-guadalupe-mountains-national-park/)]

- **El Capitan Viewpoint** (5-15 minutes) - Stop at the roadside pulloff on US Highway 62/180 just west of Pine Springs for unparalleled views of the 8,085-foot El Capitan peak without hiking; US Highway 62/180, a few miles west of Pine Springs; Always accessible [Source: [NPS El Capitan Viewpoint](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/gumo_elcapitan_viewpoint.htm), [AllTrails](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/texas/el-capitan-trail)]

## Hidden Gems

- **Permian Reef Trail** (5-7 hours) - Challenge yourself on this lesser-known strenuous 4-mile trail climbing 2,000 feet up the north side of McKittrick Canyon to Wilderness Ridge; features 28 interpretive stops showcasing the world's finest fossil reef from 275-277 million years ago with over 500 Permian species including brachiopods, sponges, and bryozoans; McKittrick Canyon area; Gate open 8:00 AM-4:30 PM MST [Source: [NPS Permian Reef](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/gumo_permian_reef_trail.htm), [Geology Guide](https://astheyare.org/2019/02/03/guadalupe-mountains-national-park-permian-reef-trail/), [USGS Geology](https://www.usgs.gov/geology-and-ecology-of-national-parks/geology-guadalupe-mountains-national-park)]

- **The Bowl via Tejas Trail** (8-10 hours or overnight backpacking) - Discover this remote pine-covered forest basin on a 9.1-12.9 mile loop (depending on route) reaching Hunter Peak, Texas's fifth-highest peak, with minimal crowds and stunning views 3,000 feet above the surrounding plain; free backcountry permit required for overnight camping; Pine Springs or Frijole Ranch Trailhead; Daily 6:00 AM-midnight [Source: [Backpacker Bowl Loop](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/trips-by-national-park/guadalupe-mountains-national-park-trails/the-bowl-guadalupe-national-park/), [AllTrails](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/texas/the-bowl-and-hunter-peak-via-frijole-and-bear-canyon-trails), [Hiking Guide](https://visitguadalupemountains.com/hiking-bowl-trail/)]

- **Williams Ranch Road** (2.5+ hours total, currently closed) - Drive the extremely rough 7-mile one-way 4WD road across bedrock and washes to a remote historic ranch house; requires no-fee permit and gate key from Pine Springs Visitor Center and high-clearance 4WD vehicle; currently impassable and closed due to unsafe conditions - check current status before planning; West side of park; Permit hours 8:00 AM-4:00 PM [Source: [NPS Williams Ranch](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/gumo_drive_to_williams_ranch.htm), [AllTrails](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/texas/williams-ranch-road), [Nutty Hiker Guide](https://nuttyhiker.com/williams-ranch-guadalupe-mountains/)]

- **McKittrick Canyon to The Grotto** (4-5 hours) - Extend your McKittrick hike an extra couple of miles beyond Pratt Cabin to reach The Grotto and Hunter Line Shack on this 7-mile round trip through the lush canyon; offers solitude beyond the popular Pratt Cabin destination; McKittrick Canyon Trailhead; Gate open 8:00 AM-4:30 PM MST [Source: [Earth Trekkers](https://www.earthtrekkers.com/best-things-to-do-in-guadalupe-mountains-national-park/), [McKittrick Info](https://www.nps.gov/gumo/planyourvisit/mckittrick.htm)]

- **Dog Canyon Area** (Variable) - Explore the lesser-visited north side of the park nestled in juniper and pine forests, offering solitude and access to unique trails; accessed via NM Highway 137 from New Mexico; Dog Canyon Ranger Station; Open intermittently [Source: [Dog Canyon Info](https://www.nps.gov/gumo/planyourvisit/dogcanyon.htm), [Earth Trekkers](https://www.earthtrekkers.com/best-things-to-do-in-guadalupe-mountains-national-park/)]

## Also Nearby

- **Carlsbad Caverns National Park** (45 minutes, 40-45 miles) - Explore one of the world's great cave systems featuring the massive Big Room, intricate formations, and optional bat flight programs at sunset; same Guadalupe Mountains range but focuses on underground caves; 727 Carlsbad Caverns Hwy, Carlsbad, NM 88220; Daily 8:00 AM-5:00 PM (summer hours extend to 7:00 PM) [Source: [Driving Guide](https://visitguadalupemountains.com/carlsbad-caverns-to-guadalupe-mountains-driving-guide/), [Road Trip Itinerary](https://www.litaofthepack.com/carlsbad-caverns-guadalupe/), [Weekend Guide](https://discoveringhiddengems.com/guadalupe-mountains-and-carlsbad-caverns-national-parks-weekend-getaway-itinerary/)]

- **White Sands National Park** (2.5 hours, 146 miles) - Walk, sled, and photograph the world's largest gypsum dune field with brilliant white sand dunes; located in southern New Mexico's Tularosa Basin; 19955 US Highway 70 West, Alamogordo, NM 88310; Hours vary seasonally [Source: [Rome2Rio Distance](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/White-Sands-National-Monument/Guadalupe-Mountains-National-Park), [3 Parks Road Trip](https://schoandjo.com/southwest-national-parks-road-trip/), [Multi-Park Itinerary](https://www.parksandpeaks.com/itineraries/white-sands-carlsbad-caverns-guadalupe-national-parks)]

- **Sitting Bull Falls Recreation Area** (Variable distance, ~42 miles west of Carlsbad) - Visit this spectacular desert oasis featuring a 150-foot waterfall with year-round spring-fed pools perfect for wading; includes picnic facilities, hiking trails, and ADA-accessible viewing area; located in Lincoln National Forest; Take US 285 north from Carlsbad 12 miles, turn west on NM 137 for 20 miles, then CR 409 to end; Day-use only, $5 per vehicle [Source: [Lincoln National Forest](https://www.fs.usda.gov/recarea/lincoln/recarea/?recid=34238), [Nutty Hiker](https://nuttyhiker.com/sitting-bull-falls/), [TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g60761-d1546683-Reviews-Sitting_Bull_Falls-Carlsbad_New_Mexico.html)]

- **Hueco Tanks State Park & Historic Site** (1.75 hours, 110 miles) - Experience some of Texas's best bouldering and rock climbing among 860 acres of massive boulders and hoodoos; view approximately 3,000 pictographs from at least three cultures spanning 1,500+ years including the largest assemblage of pictographic "masks" in the country; guided tours available for rock art viewing; 6900 Hueco Tanks Road No. 1, El Paso, TX 79938; Daily 7:00 AM-6:00 PM [Source: [Texas Parks & Wildlife](https://tpwd.texas.gov/state-parks/hueco-tanks), [NPS Connection](https://www.nps.gov/places/gumo_hueco-tanks.htm), [Pictograph Tours](https://www.bluelizardclimbingandyoga.com/hueco/pictograph)]

**Total Recommended Time:** 2-3 days is ideal for visiting Guadalupe Mountains National Park, with at least one full day devoted to the Pine Springs area (visitor center, historic sites, and trails like Guadalupe Peak or Devil's Hall) and another full day for McKittrick Canyon; those attempting Guadalupe Peak should allocate 6-8 hours for this hike alone, which may comprise an entire day's itinerary [Source: [3-Day Itinerary](https://schoandjo.com/guadalupe-mountains-itinerary/), [Frommers Itineraries](https://www.frommers.com/destinations/guadalupe-mountains-national-park/suggested-itineraries/), [Visit Guide](https://visitguadalupemountains.com/one-day-itinerary/)]
