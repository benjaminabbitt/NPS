# Birmingham Civil Rights NM (National Monument)

- <a id="stamp-birmingham-civil-rights-institute"></a>**Birmingham Civil Rights Institute** (512 16th Street N, Birmingham, AL 35203 (33.5186, -86.8148); Tuesday-Saturday 10:00 AM-5:00 PM, closed Sunday-Monday; 205-328-9696)

**Note:** Call 205-679-0065 to confirm current stamp locations before visiting. The Birmingham Civil Rights National Monument encompasses multiple historic sites across downtown Birmingham's Civil Rights District. ## Key Activities

- <a id="activity-tour-the-a-g-gaston-motel"></a>**Tour the A.G. Gaston Motel** (45-60 minutes) - Explore the restored historic motel where Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. and other civil rights leaders planned the 1963 Birmingham Campaign, which helped galvanize the passage of the Civil Rights Act of 1964; see exhibits about the motel's role as the "headquarters" of the movement and its owner A.G. Gaston, one of America's first Black millionaires; 1510 5th Ave N, Birmingham, AL 35203; Thursday-Saturday 10:00 AM-4:00 PM; free admission; opened to public June 2023. [Source: [A.G. Gaston Motel NPS](https://www.nps.gov/places/ag-gaston-motel.htm), [City of Birmingham](https://www.birminghamal.gov/government/city-departments/ag-gaston-motel), [TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g30375-d26771374-Reviews-A_G_Gaston_Motel-Birmingham_Alabama.html)]

- <a id="activity-visit-birmingham-civil-rights-institute"></a>**Visit Birmingham Civil Rights Institute** (1.5-2 hours minimum, up to 4 hours for comprehensive visit) - Take a self-guided journey through galleries chronicling Birmingham's contributions to the Civil Rights Movement from post-Civil War Alabama through the 1960s; experience the Orientation Theatre, confront segregation in the Barriers Gallery with separate water fountains and schools, hear behind-closed-doors conversations in the Confrontation Gallery, and relive 1955-1963 civil rights history in the Movement Gallery; 512 16th Street N, Birmingham, AL 35203; Tuesday-Saturday 10:00 AM-5:00 PM, Sunday 1:00 PM-5:00 PM (Sunday admission by donation), closed Monday; adults $15, seniors/college students/children 4th grade+ $13, children below 4th grade free; last admission 4:00 PM, arrive by 3:00 PM recommended; free parking behind museum via 15th Street N. [Source: [BCRI Plan Your Visit](https://www.bcri.org/visit-us/), [Family Travels on a Budget](https://familytravelsonabudget.com/birmingham-civil-rights-institute/), [This Is My South](https://www.thisismysouth.com/visiting-birmingham-civil-rights-institute/), [TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g30375-d106398-Reviews-Birmingham_Civil_Rights_Institute-Birmingham_Alabama.html)]

- <a id="activity-tour-16th-street-baptist-church"></a>**Tour 16th Street Baptist Church** (1 hour) - Visit the historic church where four young girls (Addie Mae Collins, Denise McNair, Carole Robertson, and Cynthia Wesley) were killed in a KKK bombing on September 15, 1963, and where civil rights meetings and mass gatherings were held during the movement; guided tours include church history and civil rights significance; 1530 6th Ave N, Birmingham, AL 35203; tours Tuesday-Saturday, first tour 10:00 AM, last tour 3:00 PM; adults $10, students (18 and under) $5; contact church to confirm available dates as tours subject to closures for events; 205-251-9402 or tours@16thstreetbaptist.org. [Source: [16th Street Baptist Church Tours](https://www.16thstreetbaptist.org/tours/), [US Civil Rights Trail](https://civilrightstrail.com/attraction/16th-street-baptist-church/)]

- <a id="activity-walk-kelly-ingram-park"></a>**Walk Kelly Ingram Park** (30-45 minutes) - Stroll through the 4-acre public park that served as the central staging ground for civil rights demonstrations in 1963, where students faced police dogs and fire hoses under Bull Connor's orders; view powerful sculptures including the Four Spirits memorial to the bombing victims, Foot Soldier Tribute, and James Drake's Freedom Walk installations depicting the struggle; 5th Avenue N & 16th Street, Birmingham, AL 35203 (33.5184, -86.8146); open daily 8:00 AM-8:00 PM; free admission; audio walking tour available by calling 205-307-5455. [Source: [Kelly Ingram Park US Civil Rights Trail](https://civilrightstrail.com/attraction/kelly-ingram-park/), [Kelly Ingram Park Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Ingram_Park), [TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g30375-d106400-Reviews-Kelly_Ingram_Park-Birmingham_Alabama.html), [GypsyNesters](https://gypsynester.com/freedom-walk-birmingham-alabama/)]

- <a id="activity-walk-the-fourth-avenue-historic-district"></a>**Walk the Fourth Avenue Historic District** (30-45 minutes) - Explore one of the only surviving Black Business Districts in the Southeast, featuring the historic Masonic Temple (centerpiece with former Duke Ellington and Count Basie performance ballroom), Carver Theatre, and area along 3rd, 4th, and 5th Avenues North from 15th to 18th Streets where over 300 Black-owned businesses thrived during segregation; district listed on National Register of Historic Places in 1982; 4th Avenue N between 15th-18th Streets, Birmingham, AL 35203; self-guided walk accessible anytime; free. [Source: [4th Avenue Historic District](https://birminghamcivilrights.com/fourth-avenue-historic-district/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Avenue_Historic_District_(Birmingham,_Alabama)), [Main Street Alabama](https://www.mainstreetalabama.org/birminghams-historic-4th-avenue-business-district/), [Birmingham Times](https://www.birminghamtimes.com/2021/08/historic-fourth-ave-district-key-part-of-birminghams-150-year-history/)]

## Hidden Gems

- <a id="gem-visit-historic-bethel-baptist-church"></a>**Visit Historic Bethel Baptist Church** (30-45 minutes) - Tour the church where Rev. Fred Shuttlesworth served as pastor starting in 1953 and founded the Alabama Christian Movement for Human Rights, surviving multiple KKK bombing attempts including one that destroyed his home; site is part of the Birmingham Civil Rights National Monument but located outside the main district; 3200 28th Ave N, Birmingham, AL 35207 (33.5390, -86.7982); contact for tour availability at 205-322-5360 x103. [Source: [NPS Operating Hours](https://www.nps.gov/bicr/planyourvisit/hours.htm), [Civil Rights Travel](https://civilrightstravel.com/attractions-in-birmingham-alabama/), [Save Historic Places](https://savingplaces.org/guides/explore-birmingham-civil-rights-national-monument)]

- <a id="gem-explore-dynamite-hill-trail"></a>**Explore Dynamite Hill Trail** (30 minutes) - Walk the neighborhood trail on Center Street in East Thomas where frequent KKK bombings targeted Black families moving into previously all-white areas during the 1940s-1960s, earning it the horrifying nickname "Dynamite Hill"; look for green Civil Rights Trail signs; drive southwest from downtown on Rev Abraham Woods Jr. Boulevard, turn right on Center Street near Smithfield Library; accessible anytime; free. [Source: [Civil Rights Travel](https://civilrightstravel.com/attractions-in-birmingham-alabama/), [Save Historic Places](https://savingplaces.org/guides/explore-birmingham-civil-rights-national-monument)]

- <a id="gem-see-beth-el-synagogue-site"></a>**See Beth-El Synagogue Site** (10-15 minutes, exterior viewing) - View the historic synagogue where 54 sticks of dynamite were discovered outside the front door in 1958, highlighting how Jewish community members were staunch civil rights allies who played key roles in ending segregation and bailing out arrested protesters; part of broader civil rights story beyond the main district; contact for location details through civil rights district resources. [Source: [Civil Rights Travel](https://civilrightstravel.com/attractions-in-birmingham-alabama/)]

- <a id="gem-discover-historical-trail-markers"></a>**Discover Historical Trail Markers** (varies, can be incorporated into walking tours) - Find over 100 historic trail markers displayed throughout downtown Birmingham and historically Black neighborhoods, many along the 1963 Children's March routes, spotlighting lesser-known sites and providing rich context for civil rights events; scattered throughout city; accessible anytime; free. [Source: [Civil Rights Travel](https://civilrightstravel.com/attractions-in-birmingham-alabama/)]

## Also Nearby

- <a id="nearby-sloss-furnaces-national-historic-landmark"></a>**Sloss Furnaces National Historic Landmark** (1-2 hours, 1.5 miles east of Civil Rights District) - Tour the massive pig iron-producing blast furnace that operated 24/7 from 1882 to 1971, showcasing Birmingham's industrial heritage; explore the 15-acre National Historic Landmark site on self-guided tours or take guided tours; 20 32nd St N, Birmingham, AL 35222 (33.5164, -86.7738); Tuesday-Saturday 10:00 AM-4:00 PM (gates close 3:30 PM), closed Sunday-Monday; free admission, guided tours available for small fee at 10:30 AM or 2:00 PM. [Source: [Sloss Furnaces](https://www.slossfurnaces.org/visit), [Family Travels on a Budget](https://familytravelsonabudget.com/history-preserved-birminghams-sloss-furnaces/), [TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g30375-d525168-Reviews-Sloss_Furnaces_National_Historic_Landmark-Birmingham_Alabama.html)]

- <a id="nearby-vulcan-park-and-museum"></a>**Vulcan Park and Museum** (1-1.5 hours, 3 miles south of downtown in Five Points South) - See the world's largest cast iron statue (56 feet tall) of Vulcan, Roman god of fire and forge, honoring Birmingham's iron industry heritage; tour the museum and climb to the observation tower for panoramic city views; 1701 Valley View Dr, Birmingham, AL 35209 (33.4930, -86.7944); museum open daily 10:00 AM-6:00 PM, observation tower open until 10:00 PM; combo museum and tower admission: adults $6, children 5-12 $4; evening tower-only admission $4 for ages 5+; 205-933-1409. [Source: [Visit Vulcan](https://visitvulcan.com/), [US News Travel](https://travel.usnews.com/Birmingham_AL/Things_To_Do/Vulcan_Park_and_Museum_63508/), [TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g30375-d560502-Reviews-Vulcan_Park_Museum-Birmingham_Alabama.html)]

- <a id="nearby-railroad-park"></a>**Railroad Park** (1-2 hours, 0.5 miles south of Civil Rights District) - Enjoy Birmingham's 19-acre urban green space and "Living Room" featuring a lake, jogging trails, skate bowls, outdoor gym, playgrounds, 17th Street Plaza, and 4,000-seat amphitheater hosting concerts and community events; Get Healthy exercise classes weekdays April-October, Magic City Pop-Up Plaza second Friday monthly April-October 3:00 PM-7:00 PM; 1600 1st Avenue South, Birmingham, AL 35233 (33.5088, -86.8073); open daily 7:00 AM-11:00 PM year-round; free admission; 205-521-9933. [Source: [Railroad Park](https://railroadpark.org/), [US News Travel](https://travel.usnews.com/Birmingham_AL/Things_To_Do/Birmingham_Railroad_Park_63509/), [TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g30375-d2501707-Reviews-Birmingham_s_Railroad_Park-Birmingham_Alabama.html)]

## Review / Personal Notes

---

**Total Recommended Time:** 4-6 hours minimum for the core Birmingham Civil Rights National Monument sites (45-60 minutes A.G. Gaston Motel, 1.5-2 hours Birmingham Civil Rights Institute, 1 hour 16th Street Baptist Church tour, 30-45 minutes Kelly Ingram Park, 30-45 minutes Fourth Avenue Historic District). A full day (7-8 hours) allows for a more comprehensive and leisurely experience including hidden gems. Those deeply interested in civil rights history should consider spending two days to fully absorb all sites.
