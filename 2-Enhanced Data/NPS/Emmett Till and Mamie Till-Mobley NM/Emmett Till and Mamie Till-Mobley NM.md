# Emmett Till and Mamie Till-Mobley NM (National Monument)

## Cancellation Stamp Locations

- **Emmett Till Interpretive Center** (158 N Court St, Sumner, MS 38957; 33.9710628, -90.3695869; Tuesday-Saturday 12:00 PM - 5:00 PM CT; Phone: 662-483-1231) - [Source: [NPS Partner Location](https://www.nps.gov/till/planyourvisit/basicinfo.htm)]

- **Pullman National Historical Park Administration Clock Tower Building Visitor Center** (11001 S. Cottage Grove Ave / 610 E. 111th St, Chicago, IL 60628; 41.6935524, -87.6078987; Wednesday-Sunday 10:00 AM - 4:00 PM CT; Phone: 773-660-2340) - Visitor services for the Emmett Till monument are provided by park rangers at Pullman NHP [Source: [NPS Visitor Center](https://www.nps.gov/pull/planyourvisit/visitorcenters.htm), [NPS Till Monument Hours](https://www.nps.gov/till/planyourvisit/hours.htm)]

**Note:** Call ahead to confirm current stamp locations and hours of availability. The National Park Service recently established this monument on July 25, 2023.

## Key Activities

- **Emmett Till Interpretive Center Exhibit Tour** (1.5 hours) - Explore the "Emmett Till & Mamie Till-Mobley: Let the World See" traveling exhibit designed in partnership with the Till family, featuring interactive elements and historical context about Emmett Till and his mother Mamie Till-Mobley; includes displays on the 1955 trial and Mississippi Delta community reckoning with its past; 158 N Court St, Sumner, MS 38957; Tuesday-Saturday 12:00 PM - 5:00 PM CT; tours available for $5 per person or by special appointment [Source: [Emmett Till Interpretive Center](https://www.emmett-till.org/), [NPS Partnership](https://www.nps.gov/till/planyourvisit/basicinfo.htm)]

- **Tallahatchie County Courthouse Guided Tour** (1 hour) - Visit the historic two-story brick 1910 Richardsonian Romanesque courthouse where the September 1955 trial and acquittal took place; exterior and interior interpretive displays; tours depart from the Emmett Till Interpretive Center; $5 per person; 120 North Court Street, Sumner, MS; Monday-Friday 8:00 AM - 5:00 PM (courthouse), Tuesday-Saturday 12:00 PM - 5:00 PM (interpretive center tours) [Source: [Tallahatchie County Courthouse](https://civilrightstrail.com/attraction/tallahatchie-county-courthouse/), [NPS Till Monument](https://www.nps.gov/till/planyourvisit/basicinfo.htm)]

- **Emmett Till Memory Project Self-Guided App Tour** (2-3 hours flexible) - Use the free Emmett Till Memory Project smartphone app to visit 18 GPS-guided sites in the Mississippi Delta and Chicago; features 15-minute narrated segments, historical/contemporary photographs, archival documents, and documentary footage at each location; can be done self-guided or as structured pilgrimage [Source: [Emmett Till Memory Project](https://tillapp.emmett-till.org/), [Emmett Till Interpretive Center](https://www.emmett-till.org/)]

- **Roberts Temple Church of God in Christ Exterior Visit** (30 minutes) - View the historic church where Mamie Till-Mobley held an open-casket funeral for her son in September 1955, with over 100,000 mourners filing past; the two-story brick building was constructed in 1922-1927 and is designated as a Chicago Landmark and part of the National Monument; 4021 S State St, Chicago, IL 60609 (41.821192, -87.625914); currently only exterior viewing is available as it remains an active place of worship; respect private property of the church; accessible 24/7 (exterior only); accessible via CTA Green Line to 43rd or Garfield stops, or Bus Route #29 [Source: [NPS Roberts Temple](https://www.nps.gov/places/roberts-temple.htm), [NPS Till Monument Hours](https://www.nps.gov/till/planyourvisit/hours.htm), [Roberts Temple History](https://www.preserverobertstemple.com/history)]

- **Graball Landing Site Visit** (30 minutes) - Visit the Tallahatchie River location where Emmett Till's body was recovered in August 1955; features the bulletproof steel historical marker installed in 2019; located at intersection of Sharkey Road and River Road, Glendora, MS 38928 (33.859837, -90.2759216); River Road is unpaved and can be difficult in wet conditions; accessible 24/7; free admission [Source: [NPS Graball Landing](https://www.nps.gov/places/graball-landing.htm), [NPS Monument](https://www.nps.gov/till/planyourvisit/directions.htm)]

- **Emmett Till Historic Intrepid Center (E.T.H.I.C. Museum)** (1-1.5 hours) - Explore exhibits housed in the building that formerly held the cotton gin from which Till's killers took materials to weigh down his body; displays honor Emmett Till and his mother's role in the civil rights movement; 235 Thomas St, Glendora, MS 38928; Tuesday-Friday 10:00 AM - 5:00 PM, Saturday 10:00 AM - 2:00 PM; admission $7 children, $12 adults, discounts for students/seniors/veterans; Phone: 662-375-7333 [Source: [Emmett Till Historic Intrepid Center](https://glendorams.com/), [Visit Mississippi](https://visitmississippi.org/things-to-do/civil-rights/emmett-till-historic-intrepid-center/)]

## Hidden Gems

- **Bryant's Grocery & Meat Market Historical Marker** (30 minutes) - Visit the site in Money, MS where 14-year-old Emmett Till purchased candy and was allegedly accused of flirting with a white shopkeeper in August 1955, triggering the chain of events leading to his murder; the ruins of the original store remain with a Mississippi Freedom Trail marker; located at Money Road (County Road 518) and County Road 24; accessible 24/7 (outdoor marker, no formal hours); first stop on the Mississippi Freedom Trail; free admission [Source: [Bryant's Grocery](https://civilrightstrail.com/attraction/bryants-grocery/), [Emmett Till Memory Project](https://tillapp.emmett-till.org/items/show/1)]

- **Emmett Till Boyhood Home and Future Museum** (45 minutes-1 hour) - The two-flat home at 6427 S. St. Lawrence Ave, Chicago, IL where Emmett Till and his mother Mamie lived before his 1955 murder; designated Chicago landmark being restored as a museum commemorating the Till Family legacy and Great Migration story; currently undergoing restoration by Blacks in Green organization; exterior viewing available from street; located in West Woodlawn neighborhood (approximately 41.779, -87.607); accessible 24/7 (exterior only); free admission; restoration expected to create museum experience [Source: [Emmett Till Memory Project Chicago Tour](https://tillapp.emmett-till.org/tours/show/3), [Blacks in Green Till House Museum](https://www.blacksingreen.org/till-house-museum), [Block Club Chicago](https://blockclubchicago.org/2023/08/02/emmett-till-and-mamie-till-mobley-national-monument-unveiled-at-bronzeville-church/)]

- **Bronzeville Neighborhood Self-Guided Walking Tour** (1-2 hours) - Explore Chicago's historic Bronzeville neighborhood, the heart of African American culture and history during the Great Migration era; see landmarks like the Victory Monument (35th & King Drive honoring African American WWI soldiers), the Chicago Defender building, and South Side Community Art Center; area surrounding Roberts Temple was home to vibrant Black community during Till's lifetime; accessible via CTA Green Line (35-Bronzeville-IIT, Garfield, 43rd, 47th stops); free self-guided exploration [Source: [NPS Bronzeville-Black Metropolis NHA](https://www.nps.gov/places/bronzeville-black-metropolis-national-heritage-area.htm), [Choose Chicago Bronzeville Tour](https://www.choosechicago.com/blog/neighborhoods-blog/take-a-walking-tour-of-chicagos-bronzeville-neighborhood/)]

## Also Nearby

### Illinois (Chicago Area)

- **DuSable Black History Museum and Education Center** (2-2.5 hours, 2 miles from Roberts Temple) - The nation's first independent museum dedicated to African American history and culture; includes exhibits on Emmett Till's legacy and impact on the civil rights movement, plus comprehensive displays on the Great Migration and Black Chicago history; 740 E 56th Place, Chicago, IL 60637 (41.7918127, -87.6072925); check website for current hours; admission varies (check website); Phone: 773-947-0600 [Source: [DuSable Museum](https://www.enjoyillinois.com/explore/listing/dusable-museum-of-african-american-history/), [Choose Chicago DuSable Museum](https://www.choosechicago.com/listing/dusable-museum-of-african-american-history-2/)]

- **Pullman National Historical Park** (1.5-2 hours, adjacent to visitor center) - Explore America's first model industrial town built by railroad magnate George Pullman; features the historic Administration Clock Tower Building and neighborhood tours showcasing labor history and the African American community of Pullman porters who were instrumental in the civil rights movement; 11001 S. Cottage Grove Ave, Chicago, IL 60628 (41.6935524, -87.6078987); Wednesday-Sunday 10:00 AM - 4:00 PM; free admission; Phone: 773-660-2340 [Source: [NPS Pullman](https://www.nps.gov/pull/), [Pullman Visitor Center](https://www.nps.gov/pull/planyourvisit/visitorcenters.htm)]

### Mississippi (Delta Region)

- **Mississippi Civil Rights Museum** (2.5-3 hours, 50 miles south of Sumner) - Comprehensive state museum in Jackson, MS featuring eight galleries chronicling civil rights history from 1945-1975 including exhibits on Emmett Till, Fannie Lou Hamer, Vernon Dahmer, and Medgar Evers; address 222 North St, Jackson, MS 39201; Tuesday-Saturday 9:00 AM - 5:00 PM, Sunday 11:00 AM - 5:00 PM; admission $15 adult, $8 youth (ages 4-22), $13 senior/military, free on Sundays and third Saturday of month; Phone: 601-576-6800 [Source: [Mississippi Civil Rights Museum](https://mcrm.mdah.ms.gov/), [Visit Jackson](https://www.visitjackson.com/directory/mississippi-civil-rights-museum/)]

- **Medgar and Myrlie Evers Home National Monument** (1.5 hours, 50 miles south of Sumner) - Historic house museum at 2332 Margaret Walker Alexander Drive, Jackson, MS where Medgar Evers, NAACP field secretary, was assassinated in 1963; Evers personally investigated Emmett Till's murder; now operated by the National Park Service; guided tours available; open daily with extended hours (check NPS website for current hours); free admission [Source: [NPS Medgar Evers Home](https://www.nps.gov/memy/planyourvisit/hours.htm), [NPS Emmett Till Monument](https://www.nps.gov/till/)]

- **Delta Blues Museum** (2 hours, 30 miles north of Sumner in Clarksdale, MS) - First music museum in Mississippi and first blues museum in the world; housed in historic 1926 Yazoo and Mississippi Valley Passenger Depot; features artifacts from blues legends including Muddy Waters' guitars and the cabin where Waters was recorded by Alan Lomax in 1941; exhibits on the interconnection between blues history and civil rights movement; address 429 Delta Avenue, Clarksdale, MS 38614; hours and admission fees available at Visit Clarksdale; explore the musical heritage that flourished alongside the civil rights struggle [Source: [Delta Blues Museum](https://www.deltabluesmuseum.org/), [NPR Mississippi Delta Story](https://www.npr.org/2023/03/13/1161371343/mississippi-delta-home-blues-civil-rights)]

- **Emmett Till Statue** (30 minutes, 18 miles north of Sumner in Greenwood, MS) - Nine-foot bronze sculpture unveiled in 2022, the only official statue honoring Emmett Till currently on display in the United States; designed and built by sculptor Matt Glenn; located in downtown Greenwood; free admission, accessible 24/7 [Source: [Emmett Till Memory Project](https://tillapp.emmett-till.org/), [Medgar Evers Home Monument details](https://www.nps.gov/memy/)]

- **B.B. King Museum and Delta Interpretive Center** (2.5 hours, 60 miles south of Sumner in Indianola, MS) - Dedicated to blues legend B.B. King with an entire gallery devoted to the civil rights movement and the connection between blues music and African American history; comprehensive exploration of Delta culture and civil rights history; hours and admission information available at their website [Source: [Mississippi Civil Rights Museum](https://mcrm.mdah.ms.gov/), [Visit Mississippi](https://visitmississippi.org/)]

## Total Recommended Time

**Comprehensive Monument Visit:** 6-8 hours minimum for the core Emmett Till and Mamie Till-Mobley NM (National Monument) sites across both states.

**Illinois Unit (Chicago):**
- **Core Visit**: 1-1.5 hours for Roberts Temple Church exterior visit (30 minutes) and Pullman NHP visitor center for ranger services and stamp (30-45 minutes)
- **Extended Chicago Experience**: Add 2-3 hours for Emmett Till Boyhood Home (45 minutes-1 hour), Bronzeville walking tour (1-2 hours), and Chicago portions of the Memory Project App tour
- **Full Day Chicago Civil Rights Experience**: 5-6 hours including above plus DuSable Museum (2-2.5 hours)

**Mississippi Unit (Sumner/Glendora area):**
- **Core Visit**: 3-4 hours including Emmett Till Interpretive Center (1.5 hours), Tallahatchie County Courthouse tour (1 hour), and Graball Landing (0.5 hours)
- **Extended Mississippi Experience**: Add 1-1.5 hours for Emmett Till Historic Intrepid Center in Glendora, plus Bryant's Grocery marker (30 minutes)

**Self-Guided Memory Project App:** 2-3 hours flexible exploration of the 18 app-guided sites across both locations (can be done in segments)

**Related Sites** (not included in monument total time):
- Illinois: Pullman NHP full tour (1.5-2 hours), DuSable Museum (2-2.5 hours)
- Mississippi: Mississippi Civil Rights Museum (2.5-3 hours), Medgar and Myrlie Evers Home NM (1.5 hours), Delta Blues Museum (2 hours)

**Planning Note:** The monument consists of non-contiguous sites in two states separated by approximately 700 miles. Most visitors will visit one state's sites or the other, rather than both in a single trip. Allow a full day in Chicago or 1-2 days in Mississippi for a comprehensive experience.
